Any physical [[Assets|asset]] that is used to support corporate information or systems. 
## Life Cycle Management
Deals specifically with the management of IT [[Assets]].
- Acquisition
- Maintenance
- Disposal

Plan --> Acquire --> Deploy --> Manage --> Dispose --> Plan
### Planning
- Strategic plan
- Security plan
- [[Security]] [[policy]]
- Acceptable use [[policy]]
### Acquisition
- Request approval
- Vendor relationships
	- Compare vendors
- Acquisition
- Receipt

The acquisition process should meet specific objectives:
- Maximizing value
- Reducing [[Risk]]
- Disposing of [[Assets]] in a cost-effective manner
### Deployment
- Outlining the process of installing the hardware
- Installing the hardware
- Ensuring the hardware is functioning properly
### Management
- Scheduling maintenance in accordance with supplier recommendations.
- Some organizations have predictive maintenance algorithms that can determine when purchased equipment needs maintenance.
### Disposal
Sensitive information needs to be destroyed before the equipment is removed. 
- Clear
	- Protection against simple non-invasive data recovery techniques (using software to remove data)
- Purge
	- Making data recovery infeasible (implementing invasive techniques to make data recovery harder)
- Destroy
	- Rendering the device unusable

## Class Activity 10/8/24 Answer
**What specific security threats are posed by office equipment such as printers, scanners, photocopiers, and fax machines in this scenario? Consider both physical and network-based threats.**

**What are the best practices IT manager should consider?**

One of the main threats that is posed in the scenario is denial of service. If this device wanted to compromise the availability of the printer in the office, it could repeatedly send print jobs, causing a variety of issues. The printer would use up paper (costing the firm money) and stop employees from being able to print their own documents. 

An additional threat is the violation of privacy. If the documents being printed are confidential, then that means the clients' privacy rights are being violated, since people who are not authorized to see that information are able to view it. Additionally, this implies that the device not only has access to the printer, but other systems on the network that store those documents. There is more threat to the firm's assets than just its multifunction devices. 

The IT manager should consider checking open ports on the network as well as verifying that the correct firewall rules are in place. Ports that are no longer in use could still be open, allowing outside devices to access systems inside the firm's network.
