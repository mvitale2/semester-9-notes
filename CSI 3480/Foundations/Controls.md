Tools, [[policy|policies]], or procedures implemented to protect [[assets]] from [[threats]]. They can be found in the documents published by the organizations mentioned in [[Best Practices]]. They each have their own versions of the recommendations, depending on the specific industry they advise. There are 20 different categories of controls, separated into basic, foundational, and organizational.

Controls can be administrative, technical, management, or legal in nature. 
## Control Identification
This is defined in ISO 27005 as the process of identifying existing and planned security controls. The proper steps are outlined in the document as well. 

SP 800-54, *Recommend Security Controls for Federal Information Systems and Organizations* provides a highly detailed discussion of controls. 

Some types of controls provided by the Fair Risk Analysis Document are:
1. Avoidance
	- Affect frequency or likelihood of encountering [[threats]]
2. Deterrent
	- Affect the likelihood of a [[Threats|threat]] acting in a manner that results in harm
3. Vulnerability
	- Affect the probability that a [[Threats|threat]]'s action will result in loss
4. Responsive
	- Affect the amount of loss that result from a [[Threats|threat]]'s action

Another useful checklist of controls can be found in NISTIR 7621. 

Control cost is balanced with the level of [[Risk]]. As the amount of money spent on controls increases, the lower the likelihood of a security breach.
