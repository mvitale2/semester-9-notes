Recognizing that users have certain information that should be [[information security|confidential]]. Users also have a right to know what is being done with their data, otherwise known as **data privacy**. 

Data privacy is how data is collected, shared, and used.

Organizations should be transparent about the [[Controls]] they are using to protect the privacy of their users or clients. 

[[Security]] and [[privacy]] are different areas, but they have some overlap. If [[security]] is breached, then [[privacy]] can be compromised; however, if [[privacy]] is breached, that does not necessarily mean that [[security]] has been breached. Authorized users can have access to information without the [[security]] being breached. Users can be concerned that certain parties should not have authorized access to the their data (Personally identifiable information). 
## Infringements of privacy
### Information collection
**Surveillance.** When an individual's activities are recorded without their consent.
**Interrogation.** Pressuring individuals to divulge information. 
### Information Processing
**Aggregation.** Gathering data about individuals and storing it in various databases in order to learn more about them. 
**Identification.** Using data to identify individuals based on information contained within other data sets. 
**Insecurity.** Improper handling of Personally Identifiable Information.
**Secondary use.** Information about an individual obtained for one purpose is used or made available for another purpose without consent. 
**Exclusion.** Failing to provide individuals with notice and input about their records (lack of transparency). 
### Information dissemination
**Breach of confidentiality.** 
**Exposure.** Increasing accessibility to information about physical or emotional attributes of a person without their consent. 
**Blackmail.** Involves the threat of disclosure. 
**Distortion.** The manipulation of the way a person is perceived and judged by others. 
**Appropriation.** Using someone's identity for another purpose without their consent (ex. cat-fishing).
### Invasions
**Intrusions.** Incursions into a person's personal life.
**Decisional interference.** Causing someone to undertake certain actions that would generate data that could potentially be disclosed. Influencing the decisions of an individual or group of individuals to change their mind.

**What privacy concerns arise when exercise metrics, location data, sleep data, and social media activity are aggregated?**
Concerns that arise when a user's exercise data is aggregated is the ability to cross-reference data and identify users. This may not the purpose of the data, nor may it be stored in the same database, but if the data is put together, it could identify that user. This is a huge concern because this was not the intended purpose of the data and using it in such a way would be without the user's consent. For example, if a bad actor acquired all of this data, they could intrude upon the user's personal life and potentially use it to perform other crimes, such as stalking or blackmail. Exercise data can reveal the personal habits of an individual, especially if GPS routes are included. 
## Principles of Privacy
ISO 29100 provides 11 privacy principles:
- Consent and choice
- Purpose legitimacy
- Collection limitation
- Data minimization
- Use, retention, and disclosure limitation
- Accuracy and quality
- Openness, transparency, and notice
- Individual participation and access
- Accountability
- Information security
- Privacy compliance

Key principles of the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR):
- Fair, lawful, and transparent processing
	- Telling clients what their data is being used for
- Purpose limitation
	- Using collected data only for its stated purpose
- Data minimization
- Accuracy
- Data retention periods
- Data security
- Accountability

The GDPR applies to every company that processes the data of European citizens. 

U.S. Privacy [[Law|Laws]]:
- Privacy Act of 1974
- Federal Information Security Modernization Act of 2014 (amends the Federal Information Security Management Act of 2002, 44 U.S.C. 3541)
- The E-Government Act of 2002 (44 U.S.C. 3601et seq.)
- https://www.commerce.gov/opog/privacy/privacy-laws-policies-and-guidance
## Documents and Records Management
Documents are intended to be used in a more dynamic structure to transmit information.

Records are meant to be evidence that some kind of action transpired. 

There is software available for both documents and records to help keep them stored securely. 
### Record management
A record can be in three states:
- Active
	- Currently in use to support the organization's functions
- Semi-active
	- The record is relevant to some of the organization's functions
	- Can be stored in less accessible storage
- Inactive
	- Record should be archived or destroyed
